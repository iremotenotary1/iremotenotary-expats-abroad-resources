Before you book

Five recipient questions

  1. 1Who receives the document? Bank, title company, court, school, employer, or foreign authority — ask them first.
  2. 2Is electronic / remote online notarization accepted? Valid under commissioning-state law is not the same as recipient acceptance.
  3. 3Are wet-ink originals required? Some packages still demand paper, ink, and courier.
  4. 4Are witnesses required? Confirm who may witness and whether remote witnesses are allowed.
  5. 5Is apostille or authentication required? Notarization and apostille are separate steps.

Identity

Identity abroad

International online notarization is biometrics-first. Knowledge-based authentication (KBA) is not the normal international workflow. Prepare a current government photo ID, a stable connection, and enough light for credential capture. If you use a foreign passport for a U.S. document session, review foreign-passport preparation guidance.

Path selection

Embassy vs remote online notarization vs local notary

U.S. embassy / consulate

Personal appearance required. Posts do not offer remote/virtual notarial service. Best when the recipient explicitly requires consular notarization.

U.S. remote online notarization

Possible under the online notary's commissioning-state law when requirements are met. Recipient acceptance is separate. Useful for many banking, POA, and affidavit packages when electronic format is allowed.

Local notary

May be required for host-country formalities, language, or local recording rules that a U.S. online act cannot satisfy.

Read the embassy vs online notary guide.

Format

Wet ink vs online

If the recipient demands wet-ink signatures, embossed seals, or couriered originals, plan paper execution even if an online session is available for other documents. See wet-ink vs online notarization for international signers.

Witnesses

Witnesses abroad

Witness requirements come from the document and the recipient — not from what is convenient online. Confirm how many witnesses, whether they may be remote, and whether they must be physically present with you in your LATAM or Gulf location.

Authentication

Apostille vs notarization

Notarization and apostille are different. Some foreign recipients need an apostille or authentication after notarization. Start with recipient instructions, then see online notarized document apostille guidance and official apostille resources on the Sources page.

Question Where should an American abroad start for a U.S. document?

Answer Start with where you live, what document you need, and who must accept it. Use the LATAM or Gulf hub map, then run the recipient questions before choosing remote online notarization, embassy/consular notarization, a local notary, or wet-ink originals.

Question Can a U.S. embassy notarize documents remotely?

Answer No. U.S. embassies and consulates require personal appearance for notarial services and do not offer remote or virtual consular notarization. Plan appointment logistics separately from any online path.

Question Is remote online notarization always accepted abroad?

Answer No. A U.S. remote online notarization may be valid under the commissioning state's law when requirements are met, but the recipient — bank, recorder, court, school, or foreign authority — decides acceptance. Confirm format and apostille needs first.

Question Can national American population figures be treated as city counts?

Answer No. National or countrywide U.S.-citizen figures are not city censuses. Date-qualify every numeric claim and never allocate a countrywide total to Mexico City, Santo Domingo, Dubai, or any other single hub.

Question What does an InterNations ranking mean for this site?

Answer InterNations rankings are survey popularity and settling-experience signals only. They are not American census counts and must not be published as population evidence.

Question What is distinctive about Mexico City document needs?

Answer Mexico City is a capital hub with U.S. Mission presence and corporate, banking, and family paperwork intensity. Treat InterNations visibility as survey-only; do not invent a city American population number.

Question How does Lake Chapala / Ajijic differ from Mexico City?

Answer Lake Chapala / Ajijic is a longstanding retiree corridor with POA, banking, and estate-document patterns that often differ from Mexico City corporate signing. No invented population count is published here.

Question How should Puerto Vallarta be framed?

Question How does identity work for international online notarization?

Answer International sessions are biometrics-first. Knowledge-based authentication (KBA) is not the normal international workflow. Prepare government photo ID and follow credential guidance for foreign passports when relevant.

Question When is wet ink still required?

Answer When the recipient demands original wet-ink signatures, ink-and-paper notarization, or couriered originals, an electronic or remote online notarization may not satisfy the package even if online execution is otherwise available.

Question What if witnesses are required while abroad?

Answer Witness rules follow the document and recipient instructions. Online platforms, embassy appointments, and local notaries handle witnesses differently — confirm whether remote witnesses are allowed before booking.

Question Is an apostille the same as a notarization?

Answer No. Notarization authenticates a signature/act under applicable notarial law. An apostille or authentication is a separate step some foreign recipients require for cross-border recognition.
